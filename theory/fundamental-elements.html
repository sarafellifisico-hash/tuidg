Two fundamental elements

This chapter demonstrates that the only elements that are absolutely fundamental to the universe, in every aspect, are Absolute Empty Space and Apparent Matter. Absolute Empty Space is nothingness itself, neither created by something or someone, nor emerged from nothing or from anything pre-existent, being dimensionally infinite or dimensionless.

Apparent Matter is formed by immaterial geometric elements with a toroidal shape, referred to as Alpha Components or Compalphas. These are the constituents of a Geometric Continuum whose infinite superposition constitutes a Geometric Hypercontinuum. All these structures are of an essentially geometric nature, just as nothingness itself and absolute non-existence.

Through postulates and axioms, this chapter examines the intrinsic properties of these two elements. The first property of Apparent Matter is Apparent Motion, which in turn generates Apparent Time. These properties function within the Geometric Hypercontinuum, where reality exists as a product of infinite relations between immaterial geometric elements within Absolute Empty Space.

An absolute analysis

How many truly fundamental elements constitute the universe? In other words, how many kinds of elements exist whose supposed material structure and mutual interaction mechanisms cannot be indefinitely reduced or simplified?

According to the Standard Model of particle physics, under quantum mechanics, there are supposedly thirty-four particles and antiparticles without internal structure: twelve kinds of quarks and antiquarks, twelve kinds of leptons and antileptons, and ten kinds of bosons and antibosons, followed by an enormous variety of composite particles formed by their combinations.

According to the logic of TUIDG, however, there is no substantiality and no truly fundamental granularity integrating the universe. This applies whether substantiality is imagined as a partless unit, as in Parmenides of Elea, or as units with parts, as in ancient and modern atomism.

This conclusion does not derive from experience, for such an experiment would be impossible. It derives from an absolute rational analysis: a logical decomposition of everything that may exist, whether supposedly real or imaginary, from the most complex levels down to the point where nothing further can be reduced or simplified.

Space as the absolute container

This analysis requires no supposedly precise scientific instruments, because such instruments are limited and incapable of performing an absolute decomposition. What remains cannot derive from something still more fundamental.

By this analysis, there can be only one element with an absolute character: space, or more precisely, the Absolute Empty Space of TUIDG. The word element is used only for specification. It does not imply a substantial or material reality comparable to a chemical element, but a simple determination.

Absolute Empty Space is therefore the first fundamental element. It is the absolute container of all structural elements, which are immaterial geometric elements forming what appears to be substantiality. Its properties are to be absolutely empty, dimensionless, amorphous, motionless, and atemporal.

Because Absolute Empty Space is the containing element, Apparent Matter is its content. Apparent Matter consists of immaterial yet functional geometric elements infinitely superimposed within this space.

Matter, motion, and time as appearances

Apparent Matter facilitates the emergence of a fundamental property in its geometric structure: Apparent Motion. This, in turn, allows the emergence of the secondary property called Apparent Time.

Only two truly fundamental elements and two properties therefore exist. The first element is Absolute Empty Space, which derives from nothing because it is nothingness itself. The second is Apparent Matter, which derives from no material substance because it is the product of an immaterial geometry that does not alter the state of Absolute Empty Space.

Apparent Matter is responsible for the two fundamental properties: Apparent Motion and Apparent Time.
