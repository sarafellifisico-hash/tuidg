Possible and Impossible Scientific Realities

The final chapter distinguishes technological possibilities from mechanisms the theory considers logically impossible.

Logical limits and technological possibilities

This section clarifies, once and for all, the errors and inconsistencies adopted by science fiction writers, in their entirety, regarding the real capabilities that modern science will certainly one day produce. In this sense, it may initially appear somewhat frustrating to those who believe in almost everything that merely appears scientific.

On the true teleportation system

In no other way than through a reading–transmission–construction–deconstruction process (LTCD, retaining the original Portuguese acronym, and in that strict order) could a "machine" perform what is commonly called "teleportation." That is, nothing can be "teleported" except through a total prior reading of a certain system (complex or simple, such as a person or a pencil) at certain spatial coordinates, followed by the transmission of its information, and subsequent construction (or writing) at other spatial coordinates, with the later destruction of the original system, in the case of living beings. This mechanism may be called LTCD Teleportation.

The classic idea of teleportation, so widely used in science fiction, is completely absurd, for it is absolutely impossible, in any way or at any time, to deconstruct all the Merons of a simple system, such as a block of coal, or of a complex system, such as a simple or superior living organism, like human beings or other mammals, and send them through space in the form of supposed "electromagnetic waves." In this process, the total conversion of the mass of the teleported systems into purely electromagnetic energy would be necessary, regardless of the supposed "wavelength" adopted in the transmission process.

Thus, even a single gram of any type of matter (if real matter existed), if it were totally converted into "electromagnetic energy," would have an energetic power that no transmission channel, be it a special conductor or the vacuum itself, would be capable of transferring remotely without significant losses that would result in an imperfect reconstruction of the original system in question. Not to mention that it would be an extremely inefficient and expensive process to have to deconstruct an entire system, transmit it with enormous information losses, and then have to reconstruct it again. This would be practically and economically unfeasible.

Therefore, the only logical way to "teleport" any system through space is the reading–transmission–construction–deconstruction mode, in that order. A true "teleportation" machine will consist of essential elements such as a chamber with three or six reading grids, or flat reading sensors at 90° angles to each other, arranged in a cube or box, or in the form of rings arranged in a cylinder, in addition to a supercomputer, nutrient reservoirs, and other necessary things.

Such sensors must be capable of identifying absolutely all types and exact positions of all the Merons (atoms) that constitute the "original systems" that will be "teleported," making it so that all types of amino acids, proteins, cellular organelles, cells, tissues, organs, and the entire organism can be absolutely mapped along their "three directions" (three dimensions) of construction, much like current computerized tomography scanners already do today. Teleportation, then, must be an ultra-sophisticated "3D" copier.

Thus, only an absurd, yet perfectly tolerable, amount of "binary information" regarding the positions and each type of Meron (atom) that forms the absolutely perfect image of the "original system" will be transmitted to another "teleportation" machine at another location, and not some kind of "high-energy jet of matter." This information can be archived and reconstructed whenever necessary. This remote machine will be responsible, not for the reconstruction of the "original system," since nothing was deconstructed in the first machine to be later reconstructed in the second, but only for the absolutely faithful construction or absolutely faithful copy of the "original system" which, for the time being, for reasons of security, legality, criminal and legal implications, etc., must remain intact inside the "copier machine" (origin machine).

Until the end of the process, then, the "original system," a physicist, for example, must remain inside the origin machine, whether by law or by ethical rule, until it receives information from the destination machine regarding the success of the faithful construction process of that "original system." And here enters a process difficult for society as a whole to face, but mainly for the individual who was "copied," which is the process of their own "deconstruction," that is, their "complete destruction" (their execution!), until they are transformed into a "soup of simple atoms and amino acids," which will be stored in suitable containers present in the "teleportation" machines, to be used for the possible construction of other systems originating from other distant machines.

In other words, what will happen is the following: Someone needs to be "teleported" from Earth to Mars, for example; this someone enters a "teleportation" machine on Earth, is then completely "digitized in 3D" (this is the reading process), then their instructions are "transmitted remotely" (transmission process) to the "teleportation" machine on Mars, where they will be interpreted by supercomputers, and then will be ordered in a "3D modeling" process (the construction), in the form of a "perfect copy" of the "original system," based on a reserve of diverse substances present in its reservoirs.

Thus, after the finalization of the "perfect copy," the Mars "teleportation" machine sends information to the Earth "teleportation" machine, which will only then be authorized to "deconstruct" (the deconstruction) the "teleported" individual, until transforming them into a soup of molecules. And this allows avoiding the existence of two absolutely identical individuals walking around, in view of the ethical, moral, and legal implications.

The process of confirming the bodily and mental structural integrity of the individual who has been "teleported" may be done based on a sort of intellectual challenge proposed by the system of the two (and only two) "teleportation" machines. In a process where the individual who will be "teleported" will, for example, be questioned with trivial math problems while inside the "origin machine," and after the transfer, their copy would provide the answers in the "destination machine," proving that they were completely transferred in terms of "body and mind," "body and soul."

We can even say that today's "tomography scanners" are to tomorrow's "teleportation" machines what yesterday's "vacuum tubes" were to today's "transistors." That is correct; today's tomography scanners are the precursors of the teleporters of the future. Technology will tell us that this will be possible.

On true telekinesis

Telekinesis—a compound word of Greek origin, tele + kinesis, meaning "to move at a distance"—is the name given to the supposed faculty that some "gifted" beings possess: the ability to move or interact with objects or people from a distance using the power of the mind, i.e., modifying things at will simply by thinking about the desired action.

According to TUIDG, considering the logic of the natural phenomena of the universe—and there are only natural phenomena in the universe—there is no possibility that a natural biological being, among all those we know or those we do not, could act upon or interfere with objects at a distance—constructively, destructively, or by immobilizing them—while they (the being and the object) remain separated by natural means.

According to Newton’s First Law, "every body persists in its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line, unless it is compelled to change that state by forces impressed upon it." If an object is moved in a direction, an adequate force necessarily acted upon it for a duration of time, whether through direct or indirect contact.

Absolutely all bodies must undergo the action of some type of force to be moved from their positions; the intensity and direction of this change are dictated by the Second Law, which states that "the change of motion is proportional to the motive force impressed, and is made in the direction of the right line in which that force is impressed." This acting force need not originate from the same point of action; it may originate at a distance. For example, firearm projectiles may be fired from kilometers away and still interact with objects at the target point, albeit only in the direction of the shot's propagation.

The only logical way a distant force—carried by an object or a form of energy—can interfere with objects not in its direct line of action is through reflections or deflections of the entity transporting that force. For a firearm projectile, this would require a metallic shield positioned at a specific angle; for a laser beam, mere mirrors. Even then, the result of such interactions would be destruction, illumination, or heating—analogous to an egg in a running washing machine or a turned-on microwave oven.

Levitating, bending, tearing, stretching, or twisting objects or people is impossible because three necessary factors or conditions do not exist naturally. For a telekinetic capability to exist, one would require these three conditions: sufficient energy, psychic decoders, and specific actuators.

The first condition requires that there be sufficient energy in the human brain, or that of any biological being, to promote a change in the constitution or physical state of external and distant objects on its own. This condition does not exist.

The second condition requires natural psychic decoders capable of converting subjective will—an internal, minimal psychic energy—into something capable of being utilized by some type of external natural actuator or shaper in the vicinity of the target object. Such cerebral structures do not exist in any known species, and such "ideal" actuators are not found in nature.

The third condition requires that such actuators or natural shapers exist in specific positions in space—primarily in desirable positions around the objects one wishes to modify, exactly at the time one wishes to modify them—and that they are capable of decoding specific thoughts and transforming them into constructive or destructive shaping actions. These final structures, which would need to be mobile and articulable around target objects, do not exist in nature under any circumstances.

For a real telekinetic capability to exist, these three conditions are required: sufficient energy, psychic decoders, and specific actuators. These three factors can very well be constructed through purely technological processes rather than paranormal or mystical ones. An electroencephalogram is a typical example of a psychic decoder-amplifier; a set of lasers and mirrors, for instance, can act as a constructive or destructive shaping actuator, provided there is sufficient energy.

Broadly speaking, telekinesis will be possible through a machine, such as a "special helmet" capable of interpreting the electrical or electrochemical signals of an individual's brain (without any psychic difference from the average person), converting and boosting those signals to the point where they can be used by other types of equipment in the surroundings. These would serve as specific actuators to switch on lights, open doors, or operate various electronic devices.

Thus, true telekinetic capability must, once again, arise from scientific progress—from technology and machines—and not directly from any biological being or human, as is typically depicted in science fiction films.

On true time travel

It is impossible, at any time and by any means, to travel between distinct times in person—that is, for a machine and its occupant to move from the present into the future or the past, or from the past into the present, as commonly depicted in science fiction.

Proposals for time travel invariably depend on assumptions from relativistic or quantum physics, such as wormholes, quantum foam, or the dilation and contraction of space-time around massive objects. TUIDG regards these as illogical assumptions sustained by mathematical artifices.

There are no distinct kinds of space. Space is one Euclidean expanse: nothingness serving as an inert stage in which the formal elements of Apparent Reality—or of any supposed substantial reality—are arranged and interact. Their interactions create infinitely many possible futures from infinitely many possible past configurations, all within an Eternal Present.

Space itself has no geometry; geometric elements exist in space. The angles of a triangle drawn on a plane differ from those of a triangle drawn on a spherical surface such as Earth, but that difference belongs to the respective shapes rather than to space itself.

Under TUIDG’s concepts of space and apparent time as relations among motions, the only possible form of “time travel” would be the observation of past events. It could neither alter any past episode nor provide direct observation of future events.

We already make such observational journeys into the past whenever we communicate across a distance, see lightning before hearing thunder, or look at distant stars. What we perceive is not instantaneous: the observed events occurred anywhere from fractions of a second to millions of years before the current present.

To look into Earth’s past, one would need an orbital instrument comparable to an extremely powerful telescope. Its sensors would seek specific ranges of Cyclic Interaction amplitude conventionally classified as microwave radiation. In the current TUIDG formulation, Cyclic Interaction has no sinusoidal wavelength; the conventional microwave label identifies an observational band. The detected signals would be returning information originally propagated from Earth and redirected through distant regions of the surrounding physical medium.

According to the current TUIDG model, interstellar space is not completely empty. It is a physical medium formed by extremely sparse Metalpha Merons. Their Alpha-Point organization follows the same cuboctahedral architecture found in denser Meronic structures, while differing through geometric density and distance.

Regions of this medium could act as distant reflective layers, returning attenuated Cyclic Interaction signals conventionally described as radio, television, or microwave electromagnetic radiation.

The proposed true “time machine” would therefore operate somewhat like an interaction sonar: it would detect returning information from different effective depths of the interstellar medium, analogous to the way a bat or dolphin interprets reflected sound signals.

The radii of such hypothetical reflective layers could extend indefinitely outward. The farther away a layer is, however, the more difficult it would be to recover usable information. Whether a layer with a radius of 1,000 light-years could be interpreted visually would depend entirely on sensor sensitivity and reconstruction software.

If an orbital instrument were positioned at the correct reflection geometry and could analyze a layer approximately 1,000 light-years away, the two-way propagation interval would correspond to events on Earth roughly 2,000 years ago—potentially including events associated with the figure known as Jesus Christ.

Events that occurred 2,000 years ago would, in this conjecture, return from a reflective region approximately 1,000 light-years away: about 1,000 years for the outward journey and another 1,000 years for the return. Observing what happened around that famous Galilean would require an instrument with resolving power vastly beyond that of the Hubble Space Telescope.

The familiar paradox of traveling backward to “kill one’s grandfather” does not arise, because this proposal permits only a glimpse of the past and no intervention in it or observation of an eventual future. The process would resemble hearing one’s own echoes reflected by distant cosmic mirrors: the farther away the effective reflector, the older the returned information. The text conjectures that sufficiently advanced instruments might one day perform this function.

Every idea is like the seed of an unknown tree. Like every familiar tree, it begins small, simple, fragile, and without fruit; it may later become vast and complex, bearing fruit that is either sweet or bitter.
