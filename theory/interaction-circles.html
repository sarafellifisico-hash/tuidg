The Interaction Circles of the Compalphas

Six families of Interaction Circles define the reach, intensity, and attractive or repulsive mode of Compalpha interactions.

Rotating Interaction

The Alpha Circles, together with the Alpha Point, the Alpha Axis, and the Alpha Plane, are one of these four types of geometric elements that form the Compalphas, and may be understood as the "lines of force" of an Intrinsic and Dimensionless Magnetic Field generated by a punctual "particle," called the Alpha Point. They are the most important geometric elements of this theory, for they are responsible for all processes of geometric interaction, being the functional equivalents of all the "force fields" described by physicists. For this reason, the Alpha Circles receive a denomination that is more appropriate to their functions within the context of this theory, being called Interaction Circles. In the table below we can see their names, their relative diameters, and their modes of Rotating Interaction.

Table 3.3.1 – The Types of Circles and Their Modes of Interaction

The six types of Interaction Circles are denominated according to their diameters, that is, according to their reach in space, and according to their Rotating Modes, in accordance with the type of Rotating Interaction they promote. Thus, in Meronic Construction (nuclear reactions), the Interaction Circles are the Intrameronic and Meronic; in molecular construction (chemical bonds) they are the Intramolecular Circles and Molecular Circles; and, finally, the Intraexomolecular Circles and Exomolecular Circles, which are responsible for the formation of the "electric field," the "magnetic field," and the "gravitational field" as seen macroscopically. As for the mode of the rotations of their Rotating Interaction, all of them possess only two modes, which are the Synchronous Rotation Mode, the mode of "attraction," and the Asynchronous Rotation Mode, the mode of "repulsion."

As we can see in Animation 4.5 below, the modes of the Rotating Interaction of the 6 types of Interaction Circles depend only upon their mutual relations. Thus, while the Meronic Interaction is acting in a given Meron, the Meronic Circles of the Compalphas will be in Synchronous Rotation Mode, they will be Attractors, while the Intrameronic ones, in the same bond, will be in Asynchronous Rotation Mode, they will be Repulsors of the interaction. In Molecular Interaction, the Molecular Circles of the Compalphas will be in Synchronous Rotation Mode, they will be Attractors, while the Intramolecular ones, in the same bond, will be in Asynchronous Rotation Mode, they will be Repulsors. The circles that are in Asynchronous Rotation Mode (Repulsors) may, depending on the type of interaction, come to be Synchronous Rotation.

The Intrameronic Circles (CiM)

The Intrameronic Circles (CiM) are the first (or second) type of Interaction Circle of the Compalphas; their diameters are 0.464 times the diameter of the Meronic Circles, and they are the Interaction Circles that are inscribed in all the other types of circles, that is, they are the "force fields" that are enveloped by all the other "fields." The intensity of their Apparent Interaction Force is 1/3 that of the Meronic Circle, being the first type of "force field" that is weaker when compared with the "field" of the Meronic Circle.

In the interaction between two Compalphas, of any Meron whatsoever, the Intrameronic Circles possess an Apparent Rotation of the Asynchronous Rotation type, promoting an Intrameronic Interaction of the Repulsive Interaction type. The Intrameronic Circles are, among other things, responsible for the limitation of the approach of the Alpha Points, that is, for the limitation of the Structural Dimension of Apparent Matter. If in terms of "quantum chromodynamics," these circles are the equivalents of the "electric repulsion" between the supposed quarks, while the process of "asymptotic freedom," which would stabilize the structure of the supposed "protons" and "neutrons," preventing the collapse of their structure, is due to the equilibrium of their rotations with the rotations of the Meronic Circles.

In traditional physical terms, these Interaction Circles are determinant in the process that brakes the indefinite collapse of the structure of Apparent Matter that constitutes very massive objects or cosmic structures, such as giant stars and neutron stars (quasars and pulsars). Were it not for them, the quantity of matter agglomerated in a region of space, as in a nebula, could grow indefinitely without ever producing "nuclear fusion" and, therefore, we would never have the formation of stars. And if, by chance, there were stars, they would have indefinitely massive nuclei and we would not see those typical jets of matter gushing in opposite directions from their centers of rotation, for the geometry of the first coexistent dimension would close upon itself in an indefinitely smaller dimension.

The Meronic Circles (CM)

The Meronic Circles (CM) are the first (or second) type of Interaction Circle; their diameters are arbitrarily equal to 1, and they are the Interaction Circles that circumscribe the Intrameronic Circles and are inscribed in the Intramolecular, Molecular, Intraexomolecular, and Exomolecular Circles. The intensity of their Apparent Force is equal to 1, being the Interaction Circles with the reference dimension for the others. Their diameter is arbitrary and has been adopted as the initial Structural Dimension (Alpha Dimension) among infinite possible Structural Dimensions.

In the interaction between any two Compalphas of any Meron whatsoever, the Meronic Circles (CM) possess an Apparent Rotation of the Synchronous Rotation type, promoting a Meronic Interaction of the Attractive Interaction type. This interaction is the one that occurs between Compalphas during the formation and the permanence of the structure of the different types of Merons that exist, being the interactive mechanism equivalent to the "nuclear fusion" described by "particle physicists." In each interaction between Compalphas, only one, and only one, Meronic Circle is co-participatory, that is, has its "rotational movement" in synchronism with exactly another circle of the same kind in another Compalpha. When two or more Compalphas "bind" or "separate," that is, in the composition or decomposition of a Meron, the agent responsible is the Meronic Interaction.
