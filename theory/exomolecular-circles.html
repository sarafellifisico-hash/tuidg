Intraexomolecular and Exomolecular Circles

Intraexomolecular and Exomolecular Circles extend the complementary repulsive and attractive relations of Compalphas to the largest interaction scale.

Rotating Interaction

The Intraexomolecular Circles (CiEx) are the fifth type of Interaction Circles of the Compalphas; their diameters are 24 times the diameter of the Meronic Circles, circumscribing the Intrameronic Circles, Meronic Circles, Intramolecular Circles, and Molecular Circles, and which are inscribed only in the Exomolecular Circles. Being the "force fields" that envelop the first four "force fields" and that are enveloped by the last one. In the interaction between two Compalphas, of any two Merons whatsoever, the Intraexomolecular Circles possess an Apparent Rotation of the Asynchronous Rotation type, promoting an Intraexomolecular Interaction of the Repulsive Interaction type. Their repulsion being responsible for the resistance to compressibility that gases possess when they are pressurized.

The Exomolecular Circles (CEx), on the other hand, are the sixth and final type of Interaction Circle of the Compalphas; their diameters are 48 times the diameter of the Meronic Circles, and in the interaction between two Compalphas of any two Merons whatsoever they possess their Apparent Rotation of the Synchronous Rotation type, therefore the Exomolecular Interaction is an "attractive" interaction between these Compalphas. This interaction manifests two distinct characteristics in matter, which are: the mechanism known as "gravitational interaction," and the mechanism of "magnetic interaction," such as that generated by magnets, being seen as the property of the macroscopic magnetism of Apparent Matter. These Interaction Circles are called Exomolecular because they have diameters always exterior to the spatial domains of the molecules where they are "generated."

The Exomolecular Circles are the largest of the six represented interaction families. Their diameters are 48 times the diameter of the Meronic Circles (CM). And just to give an idea, let there be two microscopic dust particles that are separated by a distance D; then, in the interior of the volume of each dust particle, the predominant Interaction Circles are the four types, while in their surroundings and between them, it is the Intraexomolecular Circles and Exomolecular Circles that dominate. Their apparent intensity of interaction is equal to 1/n (where n is the value of the distance separating any two Alpha Points), so that it is never equal to zero. These Interaction Circles are responsible for that which we understand to be the "gravitational force," when the distance (and the masses) are relatively large.
