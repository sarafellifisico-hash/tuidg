The Axiomatic Principle of Necessarily Indefinite Containment, proposed by TUIDG, is intrinsic to a supposed structural element with a quantified dimension.

This logical, axiomatic principle makes it evident that there cannot be an uncontained structural element, implying that if there were a dimensionally finite universe, or even infinite dimensionally finite universes, it would be necessary for something external to exist that would contain them and which, by consequence, would be something dimensionally infinite.

The Problematic of Ideas

Since humanity began seeking to understand the fundamental questions concerning the universe across diverse fields of knowledge, countless theoretical models have been created to explain the supposed origin of its micro- and macro-structures, either separately or as a whole.

According to the first hypothesis, the universe "Has Always Existed," and from there, it could develop in the following sub-modes: Continuous and Static or Discontinuous and Dynamic.

Conversely, according to the second hypothesis, it "Began to Exist," in two distinct sub-modes: From Something or From Nothing.

If it Began to Exist From Nothing, then it would have the possibility of being in any of the previous modes, for following the eventual creation (by God), the probable paths through which it could "evolve" could only be the same.

If matter were continuous, the universe would necessarily have to be full—that is, there should be no voids between such "being"; it would be an absolutely dense universe, immobile, and without distinctions between things, therefore without relations, without reciprocity, and without any attributes.

If it were discontinuous, it should then be a mixture of being and non-being—that is, there should exist an absolute void and material corpuscles dissolved within it, for only this void could guarantee the mobility of these corpuscles.

In this conception, these corpuscles possess a minimum and maximum dimensional limit, as does the structure of this type of universe itself, despite the logical possibility that fundamental particles could be divided indefinitely, and despite the unrestricted possibility of the existence of a void with an absolutely infinite dimension surrounding a finite material universe.
