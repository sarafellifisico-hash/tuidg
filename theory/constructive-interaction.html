Chapter

The Axiomatic Principle of Logical-Constructive Interactive Incapability, proposed by TUIDG, is intrinsic to supposed Substantial Structural Elements with quantified Structural Dimensions. It establishes the impossibility of any two such elements interacting logically without a third element of the same nature directly connecting them.

This logical, axiomatic principle establishes that the interaction between a Structural Element A and a Structural Element C, separated by the Absolute Empty Space that necessarily surrounds them, must be mediated by a Structural Element B, directly connected to both A and C. Thus, this principle leads us to the understanding that interaction—with the necessary attractive, repulsive, or oscillatory/vibrational action—only occurs if there is some type of direct contact between the structural elements of whatever exists, whether they are substantial or immaterial, for it is these interactions that are the shapers of all existing universal structures.

The Problematic of Ideas

Physicists commonly assert that the dynamic action of interaction between supposed substantial structural elements (particles), separated by empty spaces, is a "field action." Yet, the notion of an ideal field implies that between any two (or more) supposed substantial structural elements, there must always be an active force—attractive or repulsive—independent of time; hence, it must be continuous and acting upon the infinite points of space between them simultaneously. The problem is that for an interactive mechanism to correspond to this intrinsic necessity of an ideal field, it would require the description of an infinity of virtual photons (in the case of electromagnetic interaction, i.e., quantum electrodynamics) and an infinity of gluons (in the case of the strong force, i.e., quantum chromodynamics).

A multiplicity of substantial structural elements, in itself, would not constitute a problem for an eventual corpuscular structure of the cosmos; the Axiomatic Principle of Necessarily Indefinite Fragmentation would guarantee this possibility. Such a structure could still exist as being formed by infinite malformed elements of all sizes, moving away from and toward one another in straight lines as a consequence of random mutual collisions, without there being any mutual orbits between any of them. This is because there would be absolutely no structural mechanism, logically functional, capable of communicating any kind of order to keep such malformed elements bound to one another, as current interaction-field physics describes.

Thus, there would be no orbits of eventual electrons around eventual protons; consequently, there would be no atoms, nor any type of attraction, and therefore, no planetary systems or galaxies. The Problem of Logical-Constructive Interactive Incapability arises when any two of these structural elements—whether indivisible or not (and consequently all of them)—separated by any distance within an absolutely empty space and under the action of the remaining motion from the supposed Big Bang (which would necessarily exist in this hypothetical scenario), are incapable of interacting through any logically constructive mechanism. This includes any system that is intrinsically attractive or repulsive, with the capacity for dynamic equilibrium, which would promote the confinement of one element in relation to others, or any type of action-at-a-distance of one in relation to another, while they remain separated by the void.

Between these substantial structural elements, interaction could only occur through mutual collisions resulting from necessarily rectilinear movements of approach or recession, and never any type of curvilinear movement. This is because there would be nothing else between any two of them—since these would be the structural basis of all interactions—that would keep them bound to one another and cause them to describe mutual circular or elliptical orbital trajectories. This problem regarding the lack of constructivity of a functionally logical mechanism would exist between eventual substantial structural elements of any dimension, not just between those of an eventual unsurpassable base dimension, if such were accepted. Given this problematic, how can we explain the supposed atomic and large-scale structures that are proffered by current science—or rather, by current physics?

The preceding problems are derived from one another because Necessarily Indefinite Fragmentation characterizes itself as the indefinite search for an interactive cause, while Interactive Incapability characterizes itself as the indefinite absence of that interactive cause. The "interactive cause," in this sense, would be the true "fundamental particle," which would be absolutely indivisible—a notion that is logically impossible, given the Axiomatic Principle of Void-Matter Indistinction.
