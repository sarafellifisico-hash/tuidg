The Apparent Time of the Universe

When it comes to the concepts of time, there exist, across the most varied fields of human knowledge, countless definitions for something that appears to be indefinable. One speaks of absolute time, astronomical time, Friedmann time, Hubble time, proper time, real time, relative time, and many others; however, within the framework of this work, we are only concerned with its ontological and physical concepts. Thus, by synthesizing these most varied concepts of time, we find only three convergences: time does not exist, time exists, and time exists only in our minds.

Parmenides, one of the most significant philosophers to address this issue, time in itself does not exist; it is merely a result of our mind's perception. Therefore, time exists only subjectively within the human mind. There is no fundamental difference between the conceptions of time in classical physics and those in relativistic and quantum physics; all of them treat time as something that, in fact, exists and is independent of everything else. Time, along with the universe, did not exist "one day," and "on another day" it began to exist, thereafter chronometrizing all cosmic events.

According to the theoretical statements of the standard cosmological model, this supposed time is absolutely real and distinct from all things, much like supposed non-absolute space, real matter, and real energy. According to the statements of physics, time itself had a "birth"—it would have emerged at the initial moment of the supposed Big Bang, approximately 13.7 billion years ago, prior to which it did not exist. In conventional sciences, it is common to divide time into three main parts: the past, the present, and the future, although further subdivisions of these exist. According to conventional physics, time is what quantifies the velocity of all types of motion—that is, a body's motion is faster if it travels a greater space in less time, with the inverse also being true.

Apparent Time

According to TUGIA’s Axiomatic Restrictive Principles of Reality and Logical Functionality, time does not exist as something intrinsic to itself, "existing" only as something intrinsic to Apparent Motion. Time is merely a consequence of Apparent Motion, which also does not truly exist, being, in its turn, only a consequence of the infinite possible relations that can exist between the infinite distinct states of the static geometric configurations that form bodies within the Geometric Hypercontinuum; that is, "time" is merely an Apparent Time that manifests apparently in any of the infinite Apparent Realities.

Apparent Time is, therefore, only the product of the relationship between the different intensities of the Apparent Motion of elements (of bodies) within Absolute Empty Space, which, in turn, are only apparently performed by these bodies in the most diverse systems that compose any of the Apparent Realities, and without which it could not exist, not even in an apparent manner. Apparent Time is the first property derived from Apparent Motion, which is an immediate property of Apparent Matter; therefore, Apparent Time is a non-fundamental property, but one that constitutes itself as a property derived from the apparent dynamic processes of the universe. Apparent Time is illusory in the sense that we suppose there to be real differences between past, present, and future, when in fact such differences do not exist. In TUGIA, these three temporal determinations fuse into one, which is called the Eternal Continuum.

The Attributes of Apparent Time

As seen, Apparent Time is not a fundamental property of the universe, but merely the first property derived from Apparent Motion, or the second property derived from the fundamental element Apparent Matter. Its attributes are given in the form of the following two postulates:

I. Apparent Time is Inseparable; that is, there can be no distinction between past, present, and future.

Because it is merely the product of the relations between Apparent Motion, which, as observed, can be infinitely fast, then at every instant, the purported present is already the past, existing only in an infinitely small time—that is, it does not exist in fact. Thus, we might suppose that we would have only a past time and a future time. However, there only exists a future time in relation to a present; but since the present does not exist at any moment in fact, a future time also does not exist at any moment in fact. Thus, what remains for us is only a past time.

Nevertheless, if a past time is only "past" in relation to a present time, and the latter does not exist in fact, then a past time also does not exist at any moment in fact. Thus, what remains is only the indistinction of time, which we may call the Eternal Continuum. If time were something real, as physicists suppose, it would be possible for someone, at some point in a future, to construct a true time machine and with it travel, for example, to meet the moment the journey began, thereby interfering with the process, and then be unable to travel, and thus be unable to interfere, since they had not traveled. Illogical and bizarre suppositions such as these have already become classics in science fiction literature and cinema, and even possess interesting names such as "Wormholes," the "Grandfather Paradox," among others.

II. Apparent Time is Relative, since it is infinitely fractionable.

Because we can quantify and correlate numerous intensities of Apparent Motion, we can also quantify and correlate Apparent Time, and determine that it is an infinitely fractionable property. We can obtain infinite fractions of Apparent Time, but never a minimum, indissoluble temporal quantum, as physicists also suppose. We cannot, in any way—save through absolute reason—treat the motions of bodies within an Apparent Reality without including the notion of time: a time that always runs in one direction, the direction of an apparent future. However, we must understand that time is no more than a convention adopted by the perception of the motions of bodies, which we judge to be real within a reality that we also judge to be real, and, therefore, it is nothing more than a sensory construction.

Due to the fact that we are immersed in an apparent physical reality generated by infinite processes, we cannot readily understand that time is the product of the relationship between distinct quantities of Apparent Motion, and not the other way around. Thus, it is not a "true time" that exists by itself which is directly responsible for the quantification of the (apparent) motions of bodies through Absolute Empty Space; it is not a "real time" that determines how much faster or slower this or that body is compared to any other. As an example that time is motion: let us take one complete revolution of the Earth around the Sun, which we have conventionally defined as the duration of one year. When we have one complete revolution of the Earth around its own axis, we conventionally define it as the duration of one day.

When we relate the intensity of the motion by which the Earth rotates on its axis to the intensity of the motion by which the hour hand of an analog clock rotates 24 times, we can say, by the clock, that 24 hours have passed—that is, we have conventionally defined that a "time" of 24 hours has passed. If we perform the same relation, but divide one complete rotation of the Earth on its axis by 1440 rotations of the minute hand of the same clock, we have minutes; and if we divide it into 86,400 equal parts, we obtain seconds, and in this way, further divisions indefinitely. Thus, both years and seconds can be either multiplied indefinitely or divided indefinitely.

And so, what are we speaking of? Are we speaking of time, or of relations between distinct quantities of motions performed by the planet Earth around itself and around the Sun?

What we perceive is that we have a "time" necessary for the terrestrial translation around the Sun and a "time" necessary for its rotation. We can note that the notion of a year is the product of the completion of a circular motion—in this case, the Earth around the Sun. We can note that the notion of a day originates from the rotation of the Earth on its axis (one turn). We can also note that hours are products of the relationship between the number of times the hour hand of a clock rotates (24) and the number of times the Earth rotates (one). The motions produced by pendulums and clock gears merely quantify measures of motion. Thus, when we fractionate each of the 24 hours of the day, we produce 1/60th, 1/3600th, 1/216,000th of the possible infinite parts of this "time," to which we give names such as minutes, seconds, hundredths of a second, etc.

In other words, what we have is the relativization of different uniform motions, such as the circular and uniform motions of a point on the Earth's surface or of clock hands—such as those of hours, minutes, seconds, hundredths of a second, and other possible hands. Apparent motions that natural events perform contrast with one another and thus appear different. TUGIA concludes that it is uniquely due to the possibility of Apparent Motions existing with infinite relative velocities that we have the sensation of time.

2.5.6 There is no past, present, and future; everything is a single imperative

In conventional physical terms, accepting corpuscular substantiality and the motion of these through the passage of time, we have that:

If a particle P is moved from position A to position B with velocity v in a time interval $\Delta T$, it will have, during the process of change, according to the statements of traditional physics regarding motion and time, the following properties:

It is very sensible to suppose that, just as it happens with this temporal dynamic of a single particle P, the same occurs with the dynamic of a body formed by n particles P, since the difference between the two systems of bodies is merely a matter of numerical extrapolation of the points that constitute them.

A particle P moved from A to B could not be found at A, for it would have already departed from there, nor at B, for it would not yet have arrived there; it can only be found in an indeterminate intermediate position AB. If particle P cannot be simultaneously at A, which is its past, and at AB, which is its present, it also cannot be simultaneously at B, which will be its future while it is not there, and at AB, which is its present. Therefore, it can only be, at every moment, in one position AB, in an eternal present, without the possibility of being found in any past after A or any future before B.

This property, which would be intrinsic to any potential corpuscular elements (in this case, particle P), we may call the Principle of Positional Singularity, which determines that for each distinct point between A and B—among the infinite—there can exist only a single point AB where P can be. Each body can only be in its own place. If particle P returns from B to A, it can only return in another time interval $\Delta T$; thus, it returns only to its position in space, but not to its past time in space.
