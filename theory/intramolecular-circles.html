Intramolecular and Molecular Circles

Intramolecular and Molecular Circles form the complementary repulsive and attractive relations involved in molecular organization.

The Intramolecular Circles (CiMo)

The Intramolecular Circles (CiMo) are the third type of Interaction Circle of the Compalphas; their diameters are 6 times the diameter of the Meronic Circle, and they are the Interaction Circles that circumscribe the Intrameronic Circles and Meronic Circles and are inscribed in the Molecular Circles, Intraexomolecular Circles, and Exomolecular Circles, that is, they are the "force fields" that envelop the first two "force fields" and are enveloped by the last three.

The intensity of their Apparent Interaction Force is 1/6 that of the Meronic Circle, being the third type of "force field" that is weaker when compared with the "field" of the Meronic Circle (CM).

In the interaction between two Compalphas, belonging to any two Merons whatsoever, the Intramolecular Circles possess an Apparent Rotation of the Asynchronous Rotation or Repulsor Spin type, promoting an Intramolecular Interaction of the Repulsive Interaction type. It is this interaction that balances the attraction of the Molecular Interaction, preventing molecules from becoming Merons, for the Intramolecular Circles, which are repulsive, limit the approach of the Compalphas during the process of Molecular Interaction.

The Molecular Circles (CMo)

The Molecular Circles (CMo) are the fourth type of Interaction Circle of the Compalphas; their diameters are 12 times the diameter of the Meronic Circles, and they are the Interaction Circles that circumscribe the Intramolecular Circles, Meronic Circles, and Intrameronic Circles and are inscribed in the Intraexomolecular Circles and Exomolecular Circles, that is, they are the "force fields" that envelop the first three "force fields" and are enveloped by the last two.

The intensity of their Apparent Interaction Force is 1/12 that of the Meronic Circle, being the fourth type of "force field" that is weaker when compared with the "field" of the Meronic Circle. In the interaction between two Compalphas, of any two Merons whatsoever, the Molecular Circles possess an Apparent Rotation of the Synchronous Rotation type, promoting a Molecular Interaction of the Attractive Interaction type.

It is the Molecular Circles that are responsible for the bonds of the Compalphas during the process of molecular formation. In terms of "interaction force," making an equivalence with Coulomb's law, the values of the radii of the Interaction Circles determine the intensity of the force exerted between Compalphas in the process of interaction. In this manner, the force exerted by one Compalpha upon another is inversely proportional to the square of its radius, that is, inversely proportional to the square of the distance that separates them.
